Quilt is a fictional company. This page demonstrates a custom chat UI on Stand Chat’s Visitor API: comments you pin here start a real conversation with Stand Chat’s demo AI Stand-in.

How it works

Let visitors comment on your page, and let your team show up as cursors

Design tools made comments feel natural: point at the thing, say what you think, and someone answers right there. This page works the same way. Visitors drop a pin on any part of it and ask their question in a thread. Whoever answers in Stand, your AI Stand-in or a person on your team, shows up as a live cursor: it comes over to the pin, answers in the thread, and selects the part of the page it's talking about.

It's a custom chat UI on Stand's Visitor API (beta). There's no stand.js here: about 3,000 lines of plain JavaScript in this folder, with no dependencies, draw the pins, threads and cursors and talk to Stand.

Try it

  1. Scroll back up. The team's cursor is on the page, named the way Stand names it: here Stand Chat Demo, marked AI. Press C, or pick the comment tool in the toolbar at the bottom.
  2. Move over the page: a blue outline shows what your comment will be pinned to. Click a price, a plan, a sticky note on the whiteboard, anything. Type a question and press Enter.
  3. Watch the cursor fly over, click your pin and type. When the answer mentions part of the page, the cursor selects it. Click the chip in the answer to see it again.
  4. Pin a second question somewhere else, open Comments in the toolbar, then reload. Every pin and thread comes back. Click the cursor's face at the top right to follow it around.
Comments here go to site: 'demo', Stand Chat's shared demo site, which works on any domain. Its demo AI Stand-in follows this page's prompt and answers as Quilt, a made-up company. With your own Site ID, your own Stand-ins answer, your team can take over, and the questions land in your Stand inbox.

1. Mark what can take a comment

The layer doesn't guess what's on your page. A few attributes tell it: data-anchor names the parts a comment can be pinned to, and nests, so a pin's label reads like a path. data-ref marks what a reply may point at, and data-layer is the name its selection box shows. In illustrations, data-quote names the part you clicked.

<header data-comment-header>…</header> <!-- sticky: pins, threads and cursors keep clear -->

<section data-anchor="Pricing">
  <article data-anchor="Professional" data-ref="Pricing: Professional" data-layer="Plan / Professional">
    <h3>Professional</h3>
    <p class="q-price"><b>$16</b> per editor / month</p>
  </article>
</section>

<g data-quote="Sticky note: Ship dark mode">…</g>

A click on the price pins the comment to Pricing › Professional › “$16 per editor / month”: the anchors from the outside in, plus a quote of what's under the pointer, the whole line or the sentence you clicked in a long paragraph. The pin is stored as a position inside its anchor, so it stays on the price when the layout changes. With the keyboard, press C, Tab between the parts of the page, and Enter to comment.

2. Mount it

import { getClient } from './stand-visitor.js';
import { mountComments } from './comments.js';
import { buildPrompt, REFERENCES } from './prompt.js';

mountComments({
  client: getClient({ site: 'YOUR-SITE-ID' }),
  root: document.querySelector('[data-comment-root]'),
  prompt: buildPrompt,    // (label) => the conversation's private prompt
  references: REFERENCES, // what a reply may point at, as [[Name]]
});

That draws the toolbar, the pins, the threads, the comments panel and the cursors, and puts "who's here" in any element marked data-facepile. stand-visitor.js is the network side, with no DOM: it asks Stand who can answer, starts the conversation on the first comment and never twice, sends over HTTP, listens on a WebSocket, and recovers after reloads and dropped connections.

3. Many pins, one conversation

Every pin is a thread, but all of them share a single Stand conversation: one chat for your plan, however many pins a visitor drops. Each comment starts with the pin marker and the place it's about, and that prefix is the thread's identity:

formatComment('Pricing › Professional › “$16 per editor / month”', 'Is this per person?');
// '📍 Pricing › Professional › “$16 per editor / month”: Is this per person?'

// After a reload, the threads come back from the transcript alone.
const { threads, waitingOn } = buildThreads(client.getSnapshot());

A reply belongs to the thread of the latest comment before it. So while an answer is on its way, other threads wait (waitingOn) instead of catching someone else's answer; a person on your team can still answer out of order. Labels never contain a colon, so the first ": " always ends the prefix.

The next comment you pin will start like this. Press C and move over the page above, and it follows you:

📍 Pricing › Professional › “$16 per editor / month”: Is this per person or per team?

4. The prompt

The first comment starts the conversation with a private prompt: context for the AI Stand-in, and for your team reading along. It has the facts visitors ask about, the message format, the list of names a reply can point at, and where the first comment was pinned. Later comments carry their place in their own prefix. This one is 1,828 characters before that last line, under Stand's limit of 2,000:

You are answering visitors on the website of Quilt, a fictional collaborative design tool (a demo page on examples.stand.chat; Quilt is invented). Speak as Quilt's team. Keep replies short: 1-3 sentences unless asked for detail. If a fact isn't below, say you're not sure and offer to bring in someone from the team. Don't invent prices, limits or features.

5. Answers that select what they mention

Because the prompt lists the names, the Stand-in writes them in double brackets: "…this comes to $48 per month for the Professional plan. You can find more details in [[Pricing: Professional]]." The thread shows each one as a chip, and the cursor flies to the element with that data-ref and selects it, with the handles, name and size of a design tool's selection. If it's out of sight, a pill at the edge of the screen says where the cursor went.

const names = referencesIn(reply.text, REFERENCES); // matched loosely: case, spaces, punctuation
const target = root.querySelector(`[data-ref="${names[0]}"]`);
if (target) presence.select(target, target.dataset.layer); // fly, box, handles, "328 × 490"

References only ever move the cursor and scroll the page; nothing in a reply runs. A name that isn't on the list shows as plain text, and a person on your team can simply write normally. Replies are rendered as text, never as HTML, with a small safe subset: paragraphs, lists, bold, code and links.

6. Cursor etiquette

presence.js keeps the cursor present without getting in the way:

  • Between conversations it waits in free space near what you're reading, scored from the page's own text and pictures, and drifts a little now and then. It never parks on text, pins or an open thread, and keeps its distance from your pointer.
  • When you scroll on, it follows once you stop. On a phone full of text it steps out of view and comes back when there's room or something to do.
  • While an answer is on its way, it waits by your pin, and its name tag shrinks to three typing dots.
  • It rests while the tab is hidden. With reduced motion it never glides or drifts: it just appears where it's needed.

7. What your team sees

In Stand, the conversation reads like a list of places on your page and what people wanted to know about them. That's the product learning: where visitors hesitate, in their own words. From a real conversation on this page:

📍 Pricing › Professional › “$16 per editor / month”: We are 3 designers and 10 developers. What would we pay per month?
Stand Chat Demo (AI): Since we only charge for editors, you would pay for your 3 designers. On our monthly billing plan, this comes to $48 per month for the Professional plan. You can find more details in [[Pricing: Professional]].
📍 FAQ › Offline › “Can I work offline?”: Is there a Linux app?
Stand Chat Demo (AI): Currently, we offer desktop apps for Mac and Windows, but we don't have a dedicated Linux app at this time. You can still access Quilt through your browser. You can find more information in the [[FAQ]].

Good to know

  • Honest about who answers. The cursor, the faces at the top and every reply carry the name Stand gives, with AI when an AI Stand-in answers. If a person takes over, their own cursor comes in, in another color, and the AI's leaves.
  • One conversation. All pins share one Stand session, so a visitor with ten questions is still one chat. End conversation is in the comments panel and in each thread's menu; it ends all the threads, and Start a new conversation clears the pins.
  • Closing isn't ending. Closing a thread, pressing Esc or resolving a pin never ends the conversation. Resolved threads are one checkbox away in the panel.
  • A reload keeps everything. Threads rebuild from the transcript. Pin positions, read markers and resolved threads are in sessionStorage; if a pin's position is lost, it waits at its anchor's top right.
  • Nobody around? If Stand finds no one to answer, the composer says so and offers to check again, instead of taking comments nobody will read. A failed comment keeps its text and offers Retry, under the same message ID, so it never arrives twice.
  • Keyboard and screen readers. The toolbar is a labelled toolbar with V and C shortcuts, pins are buttons, threads are dialogs that return focus when closed, and new replies are announced whole in a live region.
  • It's a beta. The Visitor API can grow new fields and events. The client ignores what it doesn't know, and you can always go back to Stand's standard widget.

The prompt, lightly edited

Given Stand Chat's target audience, find ten products they look up to and would recognize, products that might have the same problems as their own. It could be the market's dominant player. For each, work out how it could embed Stand on its website in an innovative way that pulls visitors in to interact. Not just a chat button, but something novel: something our audience would see and think, "I want that on MY website." Build each one in a separate session, as its own pull request. Don't use the brand names, but make each website resemble its product in a subtle way.

This example's brief

Comment anywhere on the page. The site of an invented collaborative design tool, subtly in the style of the best-known multiplayer design tool: white page, big tight headlines, a vivid palette, editor mockups, multiplayer cursors. The whole page is a canvas: a floating toolbar with Move (V) and Comment (C), and a facepile of who's here. Click anything to drop a pin with a composer. The team's cursor flies to the pin, answers in the thread, and when a reply mentions part of the page, selects it with a blue box, handles and a size badge. Every pin is a thread over one Stand session, with messages like "📍 Pricing › Professional: …". Pins are anchored to elements and survive reloads. A comments panel lists the threads. It works with the keyboard, and on a phone with bottom sheets.

If you try this yourself

  • Link the custom chat UI guide: it's written as a contract for coding agents. Start on the demo Site ID, so the agent can hold real conversations while it builds.
  • Tune the prompt before the UI. A probe script that starts a session, asks a few questions and ends it shows quickly whether the Stand-in keeps the format and the facts.
  • Make page references a closed list. Name each part in the prompt exactly as it's written on the page, match loosely, and render anything else as plain text. The demo Stand-in used the names reliably.
  • Ask the agent to keep the network client, the thread model and the presentation apart. Here a scripted stand-in for stand-visitor.js made handoffs, email follow-ups and failures testable without a single real chat.
